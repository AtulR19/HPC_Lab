 LAB 1: MESH VS TORUS ANALYSIS

1. LOW TRAFFIC PERFORMANCE (Injection Rate = 0.02)

When traffic is light, packets move quickly with almost no waiting
time in router buffers. 

* The 8x8 Torus is slightly faster than the 8x8 Mesh (182.31 cycles 
  vs 186.03 cycles).
* Reason: The Torus has wrap-around links that connect the edges. 
  This acts as a shortcut, cutting the maximum number of steps 
  (hops) a packet needs to take across the network nearly in half 
  compared to the Mesh.

2. MEDIUM TRAFFIC PERFORMANCE (Injection Rates = 0.05 to 0.15)

As we inject more packets into the network, latency starts rising 
steeply for both topologies.

* Packet queues begin backing up at router inputs, causing buffer 
  delays.
* In this mid-range, the Torus experiences slightly higher average 
  latency than the Mesh under dimension-order routing (dim_order). 
  This happens because traffic concentrates heavily on those shared 
  wrap-around links, creating localized bottlenecks.

3. HIGH TRAFFIC / SATURATION (Injection Rates = 0.18 to 0.25)

Beyond an injection rate of 0.18, both networks reach full capacity 
(saturation).

* Average latency levels off into a plateau between 440 and 463 cycles 
  because the router buffers stay constantly full.
* Even though latency is high, the Torus handles the heavy load 
  without deadlocking, benefiting from its higher overall bisection 
  bandwidth.
